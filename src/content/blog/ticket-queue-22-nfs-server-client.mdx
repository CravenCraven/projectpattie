<p>The nfsusers team wants one workspace they can reach from any box instead of copying files back and forth. So server1 becomes an NFS server: a <code>/srv/nfsshare</code> directory owned by the <code>nfsusers</code> group, exported read-write to the internal subnet 192.168.50.0/24, mounted from a client to prove it works, and set to come back after a reboot. SELinux stays enforcing the whole way through.</p>

<p>NFS is four parts that all have to agree at once, and if any one of them disagrees you get a mount that hangs or a permission you cannot explain: the export line, the nfs-server service, the firewall, and SELinux. The fifth trap is the fstab entry, which needs <code>_netdev</code>, or the box tries to mount a network share before the network exists and the boot stalls.</p>

<h2 id="concept-review" class="pp-h2">Concept review. The pieces and the exports syntax.</h2>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`man exports\nman exportfs`}</pre></div></div>

<p>The <code>nfs-server</code> service serves the shares. <code>/etc/exports</code> defines what is shared and to whom. <code>exportfs</code> refreshes exports without a restart. firewalld's nfs service opens the ports. And <code>showmount -e</code> against a host lists its shares from the client side.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">/etc/exports syntax</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`/srv/nfsshare 192.168.50.0/24(rw,sync,root_squash)\n\n# rw / ro        read-write or read-only\n# sync           writes commit before returning, safer\n# root_squash    client root maps to nobody, the safe default\n# no_root_squash rare, lets client root stay root`}</pre></div></div>

<div class="pp-warn"><strong>warning:</strong> There is no space between the subnet and the opening parenthesis. <code>192.168.50.0/24(rw)</code> means read-write for that subnet. <code>192.168.50.0/24 (rw)</code>, with a space, means read-only for that subnet and read-write for everyone else. The space changes the meaning entirely.</div>

<h2 id="persist" class="pp-h2">Persist the mount with _netdev</h2>

<p>The fstab entry needs <code>_netdev</code>, which tells systemd this is a network mount and to wait for the network before trying. Without it, boot can hang or the mount fails because the network is not ready.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo cp /etc/fstab /etc/fstab.bak.$(date +%Y%m%d)\necho 'localhost:/srv/nfsshare /mnt/nfs_test nfs defaults,_netdev 0 0' | sudo tee -a /etc/fstab\n\nsudo umount /mnt/nfs_test\nsudo mount -a\nmount | grep nfs_test`}</pre></div></div>
