<p><span class="c-light">The ticket:</span> dbadmin1 has three asks. Onboard two contractors, one in <code>developers</code> and <code>contractors</code>, one in <code>contractors</code> only. Get ready to lock a departing contractor without deleting any of their work. And create a service account for a new analytics agent that has no home directory, no login shell, and stays out of the regular user reports.</p>

<p><span class="c-light">The real problem:</span> user management is only basic until you are the one responsible for who has access. Locking versus deleting, and human versus service accounts, are the parts people get wrong.</p>

<p><span class="c-light">What we are doing:</span> creating, modifying, and locking users, and building a proper service account, with an eye on <code>/etc/passwd</code>, <code>/etc/shadow</code>, and <code>/etc/group</code>.</p>

<h2 id="lock" class="pp-h2">Lock contractor1 without deleting</h2>

<p>When someone leaves, lock first. There may be files, running processes, or audit requirements tied to the account. Locking adds a <code>!</code> to the hash so the password can no longer authenticate, and their files stay put.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo grep contractor1 /etc/shadow    # before\nsudo usermod -L contractor1\nsudo grep contractor1 /etc/shadow    # note the leading ! on the hash\nls -ld /home/contractor1             # files still there\n\n# to unlock later: sudo usermod -U contractor1`}</pre></div></div>

<h2 id="service-account" class="pp-h2">The service account</h2>

<p>A service account runs software, not a person. No home, no interactive shell, an explicit UID.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo useradd -u 9100 -M -s /sbin/nologin -c "Analytics Service Agent" svc_analytics\nid svc_analytics\ngrep svc_analytics /etc/passwd\nls /home/svc_analytics 2>&1    # should error, no home was created\n\nsudo su - svc_analytics        # 'This account is currently not available.'`}</pre></div></div>

<p><code>-M</code> skips the home directory, <code>-s /sbin/nologin</code> refuses interactive login while still letting the service run as the user.</p>

<h2 id="ag-trap" class="pp-h2">The -aG versus -G trap</h2>

<p>The single most common usermod mistake, shown on purpose so you never make it for real.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo usermod -G ops contractor2    # WITHOUT -a\nid contractor2    # now ONLY in ops, lost developers and contractors\n\n# fix it by re-adding the groups, correctly this time\nsudo usermod -aG developers,contractors contractor2\nid contractor2\n\n# remove from a single group cleanly\nsudo gpasswd -d contractor2 ops\nid contractor2`}</pre></div></div>
