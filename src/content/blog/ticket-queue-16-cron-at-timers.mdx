<p>logadmin1 has three jobs that need to run with nobody watching. The <code>/etc</code> backup from the backup-pipeline ticket, every night at 2:30. A sweep of tarballs older than 14 days, every Sunday at 4. And a one-off <code>dnf upgrade</code> tonight at 11, once, never again. Three schedules, and the twist in the ticket is that each one wants a different tool. That is not overkill. It is the right answer to three different shapes of problem.</p>

<p>Recurring and simple is cron. One time in the future is <code>at</code>. Modern, with calendar syntax and the ability to catch up a run it missed while the box was powered off, is a systemd timer. And each of them can drop a tagged line into the journal, which is the only part the audit will actually check.</p>

<p>A cron line has five time fields followed by the command. The order is the part people get wrong, so read it left to right every time.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#ff5f57"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#febc2e"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#28c840"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">cron format</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`* * * * *  command\n| | | | |\n| | | | +- day of week (0-7, Sunday is 0 or 7)\n| | | +--- month (1-12)\n| | +----- day of month (1-31)\n| +------- hour (0-23)\n+--------- minute (0-59)`}</pre></div></div>

<p>There are shortcut strings too: <code>@daily</code>, <code>@weekly</code>, <code>@monthly</code>, <code>@reboot</code>.</p>

<h2 id="cron" class="pp-h2">cron for the daily backup</h2>

<p>Edit root's crontab and add one line. The percent signs inside the date command must be escaped with a backslash, because cron treats a bare percent as a newline.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#ff5f57"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#febc2e"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#28c840"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo crontab -e\n\n# add this line (minute hour day month weekday):\n30 2 * * * /usr/bin/tar -czf /srv/backups/etc-$(date +\\%Y\\%m\\%d).tar.gz /etc/ 2>/dev/null && logger -t backup "daily etc backup completed"\n\nsudo crontab -l\nsudo journalctl -u crond -n 20`}</pre></div></div>

<p><code>logger -t backup</code> writes a tagged line into the journal, which is how you prove the job ran.</p>

<h2 id="at" class="pp-h2">at for the one-shot update</h2>

<p><code>at</code> reads the command from stdin, so piping is the cleanest form. Queue it, then confirm with <code>atq</code>.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#ff5f57"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#febc2e"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#28c840"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`echo "dnf upgrade -y && logger -t maint 'security upgrade complete'" | sudo at 23:00\n# warning: commands will be executed using /bin/sh\n# job 1 at Wed Jul 15 23:00:00 2026\n\nsudo atq\n# 1  Wed Jul 15 23:00:00 2026 a root\nsudo at -c 1             # dumps the full script that will run\n# sudo atrm 1            # remove it if plans change`}</pre></div></div>

<p>Time formats <code>at</code> understands include <code>23:00</code>, <code>now + 2 hours</code>, <code>midnight</code>, and <code>3pm Friday</code>. If you give it a time that already passed today, it schedules for tomorrow, which surprises people at least once.</p>

<h2 id="timer" class="pp-h2">A systemd timer for the weekly cleanup</h2>

<p>A timer is two unit files that share a name: a <code>.service</code> that does the work and a <code>.timer</code> that says when. The service is <code>Type=oneshot</code> because it runs and exits rather than staying up.</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#ff5f57"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#febc2e"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#28c840"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`sudo bash -c 'cat > /etc/systemd/system/backup-cleanup.service << "EOF"\n[Unit]\nDescription=Delete backup tarballs older than 14 days\n\n[Service]\nType=oneshot\nExecStart=/usr/bin/find /srv/backups -name "*.tar.*" -mtime +14 -delete\nExecStartPost=/usr/bin/logger -t backup-cleanup "completed"\nEOF'\n\nsudo bash -c 'cat > /etc/systemd/system/backup-cleanup.timer << "EOF"\n[Unit]\nDescription=Run backup cleanup every Sunday at 4am\n\n[Timer]\nOnCalendar=Sun *-*-* 04:00:00\nPersistent=true\n\n[Install]\nWantedBy=timers.target\nEOF'\n\nsudo systemctl daemon-reload\nsudo systemctl enable --now backup-cleanup.timer\nsystemctl list-timers backup-cleanup.timer\n# NEXT                        LEFT    UNIT                  ACTIVATES\n# Sun 2026-07-19 04:00:00 EDT 3 days  backup-cleanup.timer  backup-cleanup.service\n\n# test the service now rather than waiting until Sunday to find a typo\nsudo systemctl start backup-cleanup.service\nsudo journalctl -u backup-cleanup.service -n 10`}</pre></div></div>

<div class="pp-note"><strong>note:</strong> <code>Persistent=true</code> means if the machine was off when the timer should have fired, it runs at the next boot instead of silently skipping.</div>

<h2 id="patterns" class="pp-h2">cron time patterns worth reading aloud</h2>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#ff5f57"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#febc2e"></div><div class="pp-code-dot" style="background:#28c840"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">cron patterns</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`0 0 * * *      daily at midnight\n*/15 * * * *   every 15 minutes\n0 9 * * 1-5    9 AM Monday through Friday\n0 0 1 * *      first of every month at midnight\n0 22 * * 0,6   10 PM on Saturday and Sunday`}</pre></div></div>

<p>The systemd equivalents use <code>OnCalendar</code>: <code>*-*-* 05:00:00</code> is daily at 5, <code>Mon *-*-* 09:00:00</code> is Mondays at 9, and <code>Mon..Fri *-*-* 17:30:00</code> is weekdays at 5:30 PM.</p>
