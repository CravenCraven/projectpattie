<h2 class="pp-h2" id="start">How this started</h2>

<p><a href="/blog/scraping-a-1970s-language-course-into-postgres/">Last post</a> ended with the FSI course in Postgres. 30 lessons, 351 lines of dialog, a pipeline I could run twice without breaking anything.</p>

<p>It worked for one course. That proves less than it sounds like. Every decision I made was tested against exactly one set of books. Some of those decisions were real design, and some of them were just how FSI happens to be laid out. With one source there is no way to tell which is which.</p>

<p>A second source tells you. Whatever I built for FSI by accident breaks the moment it meets something shaped differently. So this post is about the second source, and specifically about the changes it forced. That list of changes is the honest review of the first design.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="pick">Picking the harder source on purpose</h2>

<p>COERLL, the open language center at UT Austin, has two Portuguese courses I could use:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Tá Falado.</strong> 46 podcast lessons with PDF transcripts and mp3s. The same shape as FSI.</li>
  <li><strong>Conversa Brasileira.</strong> 35 short videos of real people talking. Two friends in a park, a couple stuck in traffic, a band in rehearsal. Every scene has a transcript with an English translation.</li>
</ul>

<p>Tá Falado would have been faster. It would also have proved almost nothing, because a source shaped exactly like the first one cannot break the parts that were built around the first one. I went with Conversa Brasileira. It is also much closer to how people in Rio actually talk than a 1970s government course.</p>

<p>One difference before any code. FSI is public domain. COERLL is CC BY, Creative Commons Attribution. Free to use, but the credit has to appear wherever the text appears. Nothing in my database had anywhere to store that. More on that below.</p>

<p>Nothing. A real web page always has links, so the problem was what I saved, not the page. I had two guesses: a redirect that curl did not follow, or links written with single quotes that my pattern skipped. I checked instead of picking one:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`ls -l .cache/cob/index.html\n#    -> 199 bytes\n\ncurl -sI https://www.coerll.utexas.edu/brazilpod/cob/ | head -1\n#    -> HTTP/2 403`}</pre></div></div>

<p>Neither guess. 403 means the server got the request and refused it. The 199 byte file was the refusal page. In a browser the same URL loads fine, so the server was turning away anything that does not look like a browser.</p>

<p>The well known fix is to make curl pretend to be Chrome. I did not do that. If a site blocks tools, getting around the block means ignoring what the owner asked for, and that is the difference between a polite scraper and a rude one. Before going anywhere near that line I tried the other address the project lives at:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`curl -sI https://cob.coerll.utexas.edu/brazilpod/cob/ | head -1\n#    -> HTTP/2 200`}</pre></div></div>

<p>200. The block was only on the old <code>www</code> path. The course actually lives on the <code>cob.</code> address. So the scraper uses that one, with a comment in the code saying why, and it still sends its own honest User-Agent with a link back to my repo.</p>

<div class="pp-note"><strong>worth knowing:</strong> <code>curl -sI</code> fetches only the response headers. The first line tells you 200, 301, 403 or 404 before you waste time reading a page that is not there.</div>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="pdf">Inside the PDF</h2>

<p>FSI was scanned books with an OCR layer full of guessed letters. These are 2013 PDFs made on a computer, so the text is real. Accents clean, no <code>81anks</code>. The hard parts were somewhere else entirely. Here is a piece of the dog lovers scene:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">text</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`MICHELLE:  E cadê # o Júnior? $\nWhere is Junior?\nDENISE:  Pois é, eu tenho duas meninas em casa, aí elas que escolheram o nome,\nné? %\nWell, I have two girls at home, so they chose her name...`}</pre></div></div>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Speakers are real people</strong>, in capitals with a colon. Denise, Michelle, Valdo, Simone. That part is easier than FSI's <code>A.</code> and <code>B.</code>.</li>
  <li><strong>Every Portuguese line is followed by its English translation.</strong> FSI had no translations at all, and my <code>dialog_line</code> table had nowhere to put one.</li>
  <li><strong>Marker symbols sit in the Portuguese.</strong> <code>#</code>, <code>$</code>, <code>%</code>. Each points to a note at the back of the PDF about that phrase.</li>
  <li><strong>The back pages are the notes.</strong> Numbered, <code>1. Ai, que amor!</code>, explaining grammar and culture. The dialog ends where the notes begin.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="which">Which line is the translation</h2>

<p>This was the actual problem with this source. Look at Denise's turn above. Her Portuguese runs onto a second line, <code>né? %</code>, and then the English starts. So "the line after the speaker is the translation" is wrong. Nothing in the text says where the Portuguese stops.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">First, look for a fact</h3>

<p>My first thought was the printed page. In books like this the translation is often in italics or a lighter color. If the PDF stored that, I would not have to guess at all. I would be reading the label the author put there. pypdf can report the font of every piece of text:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">bash</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | DENISE:\n/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | É...\n/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | Yeah...\n/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | MICHELLE:\n/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | E cadê\n/LHPKTE+ZapfDingbatsITC | #\n/UWYLGY+GillSansMT | o Júnior?`}</pre></div></div>

<p>Same font for both languages. I checked the color next: it tracks the speaker, not the language. Michelle's Portuguese and every English line are grey, Denise's Portuguese is black. Then the position on the page: every Portuguese and English line starts at the same left edge with the same spacing.</p>

<p>So the author never marked the language anywhere in the file. That is a real finding, and it is worth checking for before writing a single guess. When the data carries the answer, use it. When it does not, then you guess, carefully.</p>

<p>The font check did turn up one fact I could use. The marker symbols are drawn in <code>ZapfDingbats</code>, a symbol font. So the parser drops anything in a symbol font. That is reading the file, not guessing which <code>%</code> is a marker and which is a percent sign.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Then, clues</h3>

<p>With no fact to read, each line gets judged on what it contains. A line collects Portuguese clues and English clues:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">python</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`PT_LETTERS = re.compile(r"[ãõçáéíóúâêô]", re.IGNORECASE)\nEN_SPELLING = re.compile(r"th|\\w+ing\\b|\\w'(s|t|re|ll|ve|m|d)\\b", re.IGNORECASE)\n\ndef evidence(line: str) -> tuple[int, int]:\n    """(Portuguese clues, English clues) found in a line."""\n    words = [w.lower() for w in WORD.findall(line)]\n    pt = sum(w in PT_WORDS for w in words) + len(PT_LETTERS.findall(line))\n    en = sum(w in EN_WORDS for w in words) + len(EN_SPELLING.findall(line))\n    return pt, en`}</pre></div></div>

<ul>
  <li>Portuguese clues: common words like <code>né</code>, <code>você</code>, <code>que</code>, <code>tava</code>, plus accented letters.</li>
  <li>English clues: common words like <code>the</code>, <code>you</code>, <code>well</code>, plus spelling Portuguese never uses. <code>th</code>, words ending in <code>-ing</code>, and contractions like <code>it's</code> and <code>don't</code>.</li>
</ul>

<p>The words decide when they clearly can. When they cannot, the line before breaks the tie. If the Portuguese line before it was cut off mid-sentence, this line continues it. If it ended a sentence, this is where the English starts:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">python</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`pt_clues, en_clues = evidence(line)\ns = pt_clues - en_clues\nunfinished = not pt[-1] or not TERMINAL.search(pt[-1])\nif s >= 2 or (pt_clues and not en_clues) or (s >= 0 and unfinished):\n    pt.append(line)\nelse:\n    en.append(line)`}</pre></div></div>

<p>Denise's turn: <code>...escolheram o nome,</code> ends in a comma, and <code>né?</code> has a Portuguese word in it. Portuguese. Then <code>Well, I have two girls...</code> has <code>well</code>, <code>I</code>, <code>have</code>. English. Every line after that is English until the next speaker.</p>

<p>Every part of that one <code>if</code> came from a line that broke an earlier version. That is the next section.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="broke">Everything else that broke</h2>

<p>I ran each version against all 35 PDFs and read what came out. This is the list, in the order it happened.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Markers in three fonts, not one</h3>

<p><code>ZapfDingbats</code> covered most files. Some used <code>AppleGothic</code> and came out as <code>�</code>. Two used <code>HiraKaku</code> and came out as <code>⤓</code> and <code>⤚</code>. Same job, three fonts. The symbol font list has all three now.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Note numbers that look like real numbers</h3>

<p>Some notes are pointed to with a plain number in the normal font, right after the phrase: <code>Ai, que amor! 1 Que coisa mais linda!</code>. Deleting every lone number would also delete <code>fechava às 5 horas</code> and <code>continua na 24</code>, which are real. So a number only gets removed when note N at the back of that same PDF starts or ends with the words right before it:</p>

<div class="pp-code"><div class="pp-code-head"><div class="pp-code-dots"><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div><div class="pp-code-dot"></div></div><div class="pp-code-lang">python</div><button class="pp-code-copy" onclick="navigator.clipboard.writeText(this.closest('.pp-code').querySelector('pre').textContent);this.textContent='copied!';setTimeout(()=>this.textContent='copy',1500)">copy</button></div><div class="pp-code-body"><pre>{`def strip_note_numbers(text: str, notes: dict[int, str]) -> str:\n    """Remove "1" in "Ai, que amor! 1 Que..." when note 1 is "Ai, que amor!"."""\n    for n, phrase in notes.items():\n        words = WORD.findall(phrase.split("/")[0])\n        if not words:\n            continue\n        # the number sits after the end of the phrase, or after its first word\n        for anchor in (words[-2:], words[:1]):\n            pattern = r"\\W+".join(re.escape(w) for w in anchor)\n            text = re.sub(rf"(\\b{pattern}\\W{{0,4}})\\s*\\b{n}\\b\\s*", r"\\1 ", text)\n    return re.sub(r"\\s+", " ", text).strip()`}</pre></div></div>

<p>A few stray note numbers still get through, where the note is worded differently from the dialog. I left those. A stray number is a much smaller problem than deleting a real one.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">A bug I wrote myself</h3>

<p>Every page repeats the scene title at the top. My first version dropped any line containing the title. Lesson 18 is titled <em>They are getting along</em>, and one of its translations is <em>They are getting along really well, right?</em>. My filter deleted a real line of dialog because it matched the title.</p>

<p>Now the header and footer are dropped by where they sit on the page, the top and bottom bands, not by what they say. Lines of dialog never sit there.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Words that are both languages</h3>

<p><code>as</code> and <code>no</code> are common in Portuguese and English. Counting them as Portuguese turned <code>... as big as Rio...</code> into Portuguese with no translation. They are out of both lists now.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">A translation that keeps a Portuguese word</h3>

<p><code>Or pão de queijo ...</code> is English. The translation just keeps the name of the food. <code>pão</code> and <code>de</code> count as Portuguese, so I added <code>or</code> to the English words. Now the clues tie, and the finished sentence before it breaks the tie toward English.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Two Portuguese sentences in a row</h3>

<p>In lesson 8, Alexandre finishes a sentence with <code>não?</code> and keeps talking: <code>Em primeiro lugar... Então, se ela tem uma vida estável</code>. The finished sentence rule said that line had to be English. That is why clearly Portuguese words now beat the finished sentence rule.</p>

<h3 class="pp-h3">Names printed twice</h3>

<p><code>Sílvia!</code> then <code>Sílvia!</code>. A name needs no translation, so the PDF just prints it again. Same with <code>Guaraná!</code>. A turn of two lines with the same words now treats the second copy as the translation.</p>
