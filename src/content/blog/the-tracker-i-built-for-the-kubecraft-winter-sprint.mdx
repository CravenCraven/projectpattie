<h2 class="pp-h2" id="four-places-at-once">Four places at once</h2>

<p>The KubeCraft winter sprint lasts 60 days. Mine was living in four places: a notebook, three browser tabs, a spreadsheet, and my head.</p>

<p>That's not a system. That's a scavenger hunt. And of the four, my head was the least reliable.</p>

<p>I didn't want a fifth place to check. I wanted one. Everything in the same place, on one screen. Open it, see the week, start.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="why-it-looks-like-that">Why it looks like that</h2>

<p>GTA 6 finally arrives this week, 13 years after the last one. Thirteen years. That's a pretty big deal.</p>

<p>So when I sat down to build a tracker for a 60-day grind, I already knew what it was going to feel like. Loud colors. A mission board. Stars for every day I clear. If I'm going to look at something every day for two months, it can at least look like it's having a good time.</p>

<p>Here's the whole thing.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="the-week-that-didnt-carry-over">The week that didn't carry over</h2>

<p>The first version had a problem. A new week started, and the tasks I hadn't finished stayed behind in the old one. Fresh week, clean board, nothing carried over.</p>

<p>A clean board feels great for about ten seconds. Then you remember it's only clean because it forgot.</p>

<p>So now nothing gets left behind. Whatever you didn't tick shows up at the top of next week in an orange CARRIED OVER row, tagged with the week it came from. Ignore it again and it follows you into the week after that.</p>

<img class="pp-figure" src="/images/gta-tracker/carried.png" alt="The start of Week 48 with an orange Carried Over row listing seven unfinished tasks, each tagged From Week 47." loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,.35);margin:24px 0 8px" />

<p style="font-size:12px;opacity:.75;margin:0 0 28px">Week 48, before I've done anything. Week 47 has opinions.</p>

<p>Like a wanted level, the only way to lose it is to deal with it.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="the-timer-that-lied">The timer that lied</h2>

<p>The hours are the whole point of a sprint. So the timer that counts them has one job.</p>

<p>And it lied. Close the laptop in the middle of a focus block, come back later, and the time it wanted to log had nothing to do with the time I'd actually worked.</p>

<p>A tracker that pads your hours is worse than no tracker at all. So the timer got two fixes. It stopped counting seconds by itself and started checking the real clock. And when you vanish in the middle of a block, it doesn't guess. It stops and asks.</p>

<img class="pp-figure" src="/images/gta-tracker/away.png" alt="The Grind Timer showing a pink prompt that reads: You were away 2 hours 11 minutes, log it? with Log It and Skip buttons." loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;display:block;border-radius:8px;border:1px solid rgba(128,128,128,.35);margin:24px 0 8px" />

<p style="font-size:12px;opacity:.75;margin:0 0 28px">Two hours and eleven minutes of deep focus. Sure.</p>

<p>You decide what counts.</p>

<h2 class="pp-h2" id="making-it-yours">Making it yours</h2>

<p>My own copy writes everything to a spreadsheet, so I end up with real data from the sprint. That's great for me and useless for you, because it's my spreadsheet.</p>

<p>So the version on this site is cut loose from my stuff. It's one page. There's no sign in. It saves in your own browser, and an Export button hands you a spreadsheet file whenever you want one.</p>

<p>It opens with a sample week so there's something to click. After that it's yours: rename the categories, add your own, set your weekly hours goal, and hit Start Fresh when you're done playing with my samples.</p>
